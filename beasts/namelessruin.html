The Nameless Ruin, born with the name Cein Ād’belōr (meaning “Great Below”), is the child of the second highruler, Lord As'deghorath, the precursor of the semi-ratenous beings (of neither true birth nor among the lesser category), and the Harbinger of Erosene, or the End Times for all humanity and mortal kind. It is depicted as an unstoppable, six-legged mass of monstrous proportions, and is capable of both sundering the deep earth and rendering fierce storms null. Although mistaken as a ferron, the terror is a mandragora which carries upon its body the Valus Worldcutter, an immense and emburdening form of armor forged by the realmic daemons as both safety and sentence. The Valus Worldcutter is always depicted along with Cein Ād’belōr, and is what lends to the beast’s mast-like head and stern-like body.

(For information of prophecy, see below)

Sometime within the Age of Solset, it was believed that a brief period of ethereal but unyielding darkness manifested and claimed a significant portion of the World’s surface, effectively dividing all life and land between two halves upon the Sunlands. This event was known as the Black Eclipse, or the God-wall, in reference to its linear, wall-like shape, and it allowed a brief period called the Age of the Schism in which the demons of the Realm could reign terror wherever the God-wall stretched. At the time, no being was thought capable of summoning such a catastrophe—save one, whose name was not spoken, and whose shadow already laid across the world before: the Black Harbinger. It is believed that the Age of the Schism—though brief—was but a test by the realmic Lord to gauge both the power of the then young beast and the aftermath that would ensue in the wake of its path. Such theories are only supported with the notion that the Age of the Schism only occurred once, and no more.

The name Cein Ād’belōr originates in likeness to an older power, a god spoken of in fragments older than the daemons themselves, and which drove them to inhabit the Realm. It ruled over an era with no crown, but as a presence in likeness to a divine being, and was given the name Cein Ād’chasor the Unreachable. Though never fully accounted for nor explored, the god is described as bearing 'burning hands and an eternal veil, with a Worldly body of white.’ Both omnipotent and omnipresent, its rule has no documented history, for nothing that endured its time remained to bear witness to it directly, but only in reference. In rebellion to its power, Cein Ād’belōr was created in defiance against the Unreachable, and was fashioned to bear a mantle against its wrath and reign. What followed is left to inference, for it is said that the beast was not meant merely as a shield for the World, but to endure the god’s presence and power—and, in time, to subdue it—and take away its sway of influence so that the very skies and the residents of the Realm may no longer be at any god’s mercy.

In myth, Cein Ād’belōr is thought to be born a dragon, however the Lords of the Realm and their power perfected the beast’s body through rituals and rites to better fit their goal. These rituals were described as involving the most powerful of the daemons, among the Lord, with a massive collective effort. From a verse of the Revelations of Uranax:

"A gale shrilled and battered against the confines of their hidden World; from atop their warm calcified remains of ancestors, and from below the shell of the heart’s head. The Lord spoke, and the highdrove bowed. For first their Might said to take the chaos which whirled beneath and behind the heart. It was weak, too unruly, and being so, would never gain the genesis of guiding a wake where it should be guided. For the Lord knew that in chaos was its power not in its destruction, but in its eternality.

The highdrove bowed, and in Will they made it so.

Next was its crown, which threatened the promise of earth bones, and dwarfed the palace of lineage. They listened again, and bowed again. For he said to take the pride which spread a finite distance. It was trapped, too contained, and in its imprisonment would never gain the perfection of challenging the order of their Unreachable homes. For the Lord knew that in pride was its hunger inspired not in purpose, but in promise.

The highdrove bowed, and in Will they made it so.

And when they asked where the power of chaos would be kept, and where its hunger of pride would be hailed from, their Lord spoke again in but a single word: behold."

Cein Ād’belōr’s words of might were captured and shifted, rendering its breath silent and null, and the daemons split its pride, or wings from its back, leaving him flightless. In its place, its creators had instead changed its body, gifting to it another pair of limbs to support its enormity, and a scabrously jagged and impenetrable skin. The Lord battled it for a lustrum, and finally, bestowed a cleaving down the ridge of its back.

The changes made were with purpose; the nature of breath—commonly manifesting as one of the basic elements (fire, chillwind, resonance, and air)—contains a chaos and destruction limited to the properties of the World and reality. It was repurposed, changed in its form and presence to endlessly release from its body in the wake of the Lord’s scar. Wherever it walked, a World encumbering darkness followed; a darkness filled with an accursed myst capable of degrading all life and living, and absorbing the light of the sun, giving it extremely pernicious properties. Thus, within archives, versus, and stories told, its presence became less a name than an omen. Dread followed the beast through all tellings, one akin to one of the Great Sorrows, and within it mortals found a reflection of their own helplessness. This alone gave a fear enough that many would not speak its name, and thus, the title Nameless Ruin followed in spite of it.

In prophecy, myth, stories and fears do they share a commonality: that Cein Ād’belōr represents inevitability, and the misery it entails in its wake. Sometime in the far-future, the beast would reawaken, and in its return would arouse another God-wall—but unlike before, a more true one. Not as a test, but in its full and final making to bring about the final era.
