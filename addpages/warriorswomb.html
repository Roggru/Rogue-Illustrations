The Warrior's Womb

Within the Hearthcity of Aldin Arae was the World’s greatest warrior: Meriden of the Golden Geirendaul, who led the grand city's armies and its valor. The collective of such a force, which were together known as the Orahkan, or the Outer Vanguard, eventually would number into the tens of thousands.

Prior to the Titanosul, or the nine-year Titan War, the Outer Vanguard existed in its infancy, mostly consisting of young sprouts from the Hearthcity who were noted to have more refined ardor than usual. Spiritual and divine in power, they defied the physicality of the World enough that they assumed no shape, and instead adapted themselves as the situation needed to undertake any bodily form. This ability granted them a considerable edge over their foes—namely the Shades of Kurne, a long and unending enemy of the Hearthcity that preceded the First Dawn. For every battle, the Vanguard were able to better shape themselves and coalesce their ardor enough to become frighteningly capable warriors of the auroral city, though were ultimately unable to completely vanquish the shades altogether.

Eventually, the ethereal fight amassed no new conclusions, gradually slowing to a stalemate between the two sides, albeit one that still consumed many while it occurred. Dissatisfied with the outcome, Meriden returned to Aldin Arae to seek the Golden Geirendaul—a divine forge that both preserved the light of the city and shaped the immaterial to be tangible—and shape an artifact. It created a spear, the Razhierre, a weapon capable of suffusing Meriden's ardor into a fine point which could be hurled as powerfully as a falling star, but at the cost of becoming vulnerable without it present to protect him. As a result, Meriden would explore alternate ways to wage battle, at some point surmising of the Warding Path, a philosophy and system of power that focused on the presence of ardor and its role in safeguarding its possessor by means of induration. Returning to the Titan War, he would find his logic sound when he felled Uvasis, titan of the Low Steppes. From its body, Meriden would take physical parts, recrafting and repurposing them as armor in which his ardor could reinforce to be nearly impenetrable. Likewise, the Vanguard partook in this, naming the process as honor-housing (to bear battle). It would become tradition after every victory, and made permanent the vows of the Vanguard to war forever as their bodies became shaped, real and unchanging. In time, the warriors would metamorphosize entirely, some—known as the Varielle—becoming embodiments of defense like walls, towers, salients and bridges for the Hearthcity, while some became monoliths and obelisks, guidemarks for their sibling Children.

Over the Titanosul's years, nearly a dozen titans would fall, spread between notable battles. Their enormity apparent, most of the Orahkan became emburdened as a result of the voluminous takings, and were sworn to encircle the auroral city with immense and ornate walls. Some others, however, became receptive to the Styria, a strange force leftover by the titan's power which influenced the Orahkan to harbor uncontainable feelings of excitement, often manifesting in various ways such as anger, restlessness, and mania. This would divide the Vanguard by nature of polarity, creating two distinct martial paths, of the Varielle and their immortal shell, and the newly appointed Idrelun, who prioritized the way of an unstoppable brand. While Meriden contributed much in the string of victories against the titans, the styrian's schism affected the Vanguard's coherence, becoming more apparent as each titan fell.

Due to the emburdening consequence of honor-housing, Meriden would regretfully reject all spoils he was meant to carry due to his status as leader of the Vanguard. Eventually, he would select a hundred of the Idrelun to share the burden as a means to both spare them from becoming sworn as the immovable Varielle, or their excited Idrelun siblings. Naming them as the Anointed Spearpoint, these hundred warriors were trusted to abandon the honor-housing as their leader did, to accompany him across Llys Apeiron and put an end to the Titanosul. It would be a monumental endeavor, a deeply sacred campaign to vanquish their enemy sovereign and her titans which would shape the World for every one slain.

And swift was their valor, imposing was their wake of war. Across their battles, Meriden would waver beneath the weight of forgoing honor-housing. As a means to atone, to honor in his own way, Meriden began to shape himself—removing and taking away one piece for every shade and titan slain instead of taking one. Significant in its own way, eventually the shaping encroached against the edge of his frame. Meriden chose to persevere, continuing as a vow which led to his amputation of his limbs and organs. He would become susceptible to the styrian's force, even more than the Razhierre left him. The hundred Anointed, who were masters at their art of war now, would periodically sacrifice themselves to mend and reshape the general warrior through ardor. Though effectively facilitating the end of the Titanosul, it prolonged the weight of the war, making every subsequent excursion more difficult. Every Anointed would offer themself in time, until its end to the very last.

Upon the final confrontation of the war, Meriden would reach their sovereign, the Mothermaer Syr Bia’en. Their battle proved to be devastating in strength, and attritious in length. Her step rang at the World like a bell, which struck fear into the Children of the Hearthcity and weakened the earth beneath. The duel lasted an untold amount of days, until Syr Bia’en struck at the World one last time and caused an enormous collapse that swallowed the two. Trapped deep within, their final stand would occur when Meriden threw the Razhierre through the Mothermaer's chest, slaying her but incapacitating him. Though he tried to hold the sovereign's corpse, her enormity proved too much, and she would fall against the World once more before the next daybreak. The ringing of the World signaled the end to the Titanosul. Believing there was no just way to accept the outcome, he would honor his shaping vow for the last time, removing the only equitable part that remained: his heart. It would seal his fate, and leave him to a fading death which he would honor those that honored and offered to him; the entirety of the Outer Vanguard, and the hundred Anointed Spearpoint.

Yet the offering of his heart was not entirely met, as within the aftermath of the great fight, the Mothermaer's body still contained a styrian force too great to suppress or end. It was strong enough to create eroding winds, carving away the earth into a hollow shape which her body filled. The last of her calls—echoing and shrilling still—rang within the hollow of the World and could not escape. There was no peace, no void that graced the warrior's cessation, and in time, the body of Syr Bia’en would form a shrine that bowed to the general warrior, hands clasped to form an enclosed barrier which made the styrian force empower the victor. From his regret did it erode the layers that shaped him. From honor did it strip the voice he commanded with. From his heart did it fill all that he gave away, and remade the warrior once more. Renewed, returned, reborn.
